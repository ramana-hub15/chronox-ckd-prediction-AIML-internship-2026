Understanding Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD)

Chronic Kidney Disease is a gradual loss of kidney filtration capacity over three months or longer. Because early stages often produce no symptoms, biomarker screening is critical.

Major Risk Factors
  • Hypertension: High blood pressure damages delicate glomerular capillaries over time.
  • Diabetes Mellitus: Elevated blood glucose accelerates diabetic nephropathy.
  • Proteinuria / Albuminuria: Persistent urine albumin is an early warning sign of filtration barrier injury.
  • Family History & Age: Risk increases after age 50 or with inherited kidney conditions.
  • Frequent NSAID Use: Long-term over-the-counter painkillers can impair renal perfusion.

Evidence-Based Prevention Tips
  • Keep blood pressure controlled (typically < 130/80 mmHg as advised by your doctor).
  • Manage HbA1c and fasting/random glucose if living with diabetes.
  • Reduce excess dietary sodium (< 2,000 mg/day) and ultra-processed foods.
  • Stay physically active (150 minutes/week) and avoid tobacco.
  • Ask your clinician for routine Serum Creatinine (eGFR) and Urine Albumin checks.
